Photographing Your Shelves

A one-minute guide to getting clean, accurate results from the Spine Scanner. Better photos mean fewer corrections later.

1 How to take the photo

Do

  • Stand straight in front of the shelf, camera level with the spines.
  • Fill the frame with one shelf (or one section) at a time.
  • Turn on the lights; daylight or bright room light is best.
  • Hold steady and let the camera focus before you tap.
  • Keep spines upright so the text reads left-to-right.

Skip

  • Angled or tilted shots — text on the ends gets distorted.
  • Whole bookcases from across the room; spines get too small.
  • Glare from a window or flash washing out the titles.
  • Blurry or motion shots — a fuzzy title can't be read.
  • Books lying flat or stacked; scan them standing up.

2 What to expect

📖

Titles & authors

Read directly from the spines. Clear, upright text comes through very reliably.

🔢

ISBN, publisher, year

Looked up automatically for each book. Most well-known titles fill in; rarer ones may not.

📝

An editable table

Every result appears in a table you can fix before exporting to a spreadsheet.

3 Why some rows are flagged

The scanner is honest about uncertainty. When a spine is partly hidden, worn, or hard to read — or when an ISBN is a best guess — that row is flagged with a note in the "Confidence notes" column so you know exactly what to double-check. A flag isn't an error; it's a heads-up. Always give flagged rows a quick look before you rely on the catalog.

4 Start to finish

  1. Open the Spine Scanner link and enter your access code.
  2. Take or upload one photo per shelf (add several at once if you like).
  3. Tap Scan spines and wait a few seconds per photo.
  4. Review the table — fix any flagged rows, add notes if needed.
  5. Tap Download CSV and open it in Google Sheets.
